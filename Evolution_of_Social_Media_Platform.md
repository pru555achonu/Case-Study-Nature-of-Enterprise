## Historical Development

*Timeline and Milestones:* <br>
2003 - MySpace launches:
MySpace was launched in August 2003 by Tom Anderson and Chris Dewolfe. It quickly became one of the most popular social networking sites in the world.
Users could create their own profiles, add music they liked, and best of all, connect with their friends.
It was a massive force in the music industry, and it was getting massive numbers of independent artists out there.
It was the number one social networking site in the entire world from 2005 to 2008.

2004 - Facebook changes social networking: <br>
Facebook was launched on the 4th of February in 2004, by Mark Zuckerberg while he was a student at Harvard University.
It was initially a college student-only site before expanding to the public.
It was far cleaner than MySpace, with real names and a much easier way of networking.
By 2008, it had surpassed MySpace in terms of popularity and had become the number one social networking site in the world.

2010 - Instagram introduces visual sharing: <br>
Instagram was launced on october 2010 by Kevin Systrom and Mike Krieger.
It was initially a photo-sharing app for phones, making social media extremely visual.
filters, hashtags, stories, and reels were all key features that helped become one of the most used platforms with the younger generation and with businesses.
Its success was a clear indicator of the relevance of a smartphone and visual content in today's society.

2016- TikTok revolutionises short videos: <br>
TikTok was launched internationally in 2019 by a Chinese company called ByteDance.
It became a well known platform for short videos, and it allowed people to go viral quickly.
It was very much a platform of content discovery rather than one of friends and followers.
The power of the algorithm meant that anyone could go viral, and it completely changed the way content was created and consumed online.
Content creators were now catching on to making money from TikTok, the algorithm proved that real money could be made with the amount of views being generated from one short video.

## Business view point analysis:

With the evolution of social media, it came with impacts on how companies run their businesses and how society functions around the usage of social media.
For businesses, social media brought new ways to advertise and sell products for their customers to buy. On the societal aspect, it was able to allow
people new ways to communicate with each other, as well as making it easier to talk to other people from around the globe. Right now, we are going to be looking at
the business aspect of the evolution of social media, to see how companies were able to adapt to the ever-changing land scape of social media business.

A way that major brands were able to bring more attention to their business was through advertisements on social media platforms. Advertisements on social
media was introduced as early as 2006 with Facebook, after that was Twitter enabling ads in 2010, then other sites such as Snapchat, YouTube and more adding
ads to their platforms. (Maryville University, 2020). Companies saw this as an opportunity to make their brands more popular, so they started to use social media as a way to grow. So, they
started putting ads on these sites either in picture or video form to inform people about their products or recruit them for their company. Even making
some of the ads entertaining to some degree to entice people. Other social media sites even created accounts on their competitors' websites to advertise and talk about upcoming updates to their own platforms.

According to Yoesoep Edhie Rachmad on his book 'Like, Share, Sell: Social Media for the Aspiring Business Owner,' he explains how the rise of social media has
given businesses the ability to look at real time data of their customers to get a good idea of topics such as "engagement rates, follower demographics, and
content reach" to truly understand their audience and use that data to help improve aspects of their company to bring more success to the business as
a whole. (Rachmad, 2025). Despite these features being able to massively help business owners, including some on the smaller side of the spectrum, he does warn to not put too 
much reliance on quantitative metrics. These sets of data do not capture the heart and soul of human connection. Which he argues that the success of social media
mainly comes from the ability to listen, respond, and adapt in ways that corresponds to the ever-changing interests or demands of their audience. (Rachmad, 2025).

With social media rapidly being used more as an essential tool for companies to grow their businesses, this brought along an increased need for said businesses to 
implement an all-in-one solution for their growing social media presence. So, a way that companies chose to deal with this is by implementing Sprinklr, which is
an AI native SaaS enterprise software company that can allow brands to manage everything about their social media strategy in "one complete system". (Allant Group, 2018). With this
all-in-one system, comes with advanced & customizable governance workflows, asset management, monitoring, audience profiling, campaign execution, and analytics.
These solutions can help any brand achieve their strategic goals without much push back or hassle thanks to Sprinklr. These tools can help assist marketers as 
well, with their goal towards an Omi-channel ecosystem, which is when a business's social media is directly connected to website, email content, online 
reputation management, and even offline channels. This social data will allow brands to more accurately research and show their new products, while also being
able to predict a crisis in customer sentiment. (Allant Group, 2018).

The impact of the evolution of social media has had a massive on the business side of the world. As we have seen, there has been many different strategies and tips
that have been used by businesses to help gain their companies more popularity and success to their brand. Throughout the years, we have seen these companies 
reach the peak of their popularity, thanks to social media, with some businesses gaining more employees, more eyes on their products, and becoming some of 
the biggest names in their fields such as Duolingo and Ryanair.


## Societal view point analysis:

Social media has evolved from personal communication into an essential aspect of people’s lives. Research shows that 4.88 billion people use social media worldwide, representing 60.6% of the global population. The most popular social media platforms include Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok, which have at least one billion active users monthly(Kemp, 2023).<br> 

Social media platforms facilitate users many opportunities such as expressing their identity, connecting with people, interacting with others, validating by blue marks or likes from followers. In addition, people can connect regardless of geographical distance or different time zones, which is crucial for social interaction (Kietzmann et al., 2011). These benefits make users enjoy using social media. Many people have become social influencers and entrepreneurs by taking advantage of these opportunities, earning income through social media platforms in ways that were not possible in the past.<br>

Although social media offers many benefits, it can also have a negative impact on society if the user is not using it wisely. It can psychologically influence people's sense of belonging and validation(Nadkarni & Hofmann, 2012). People often seek validation in the form of blue marks or likes from followers. Many studies show that the number of likes and comments can influence people in various ways, such as emotions, self-esteem, and social behaviour. For instance, when the poster receives high numbers of likes and comments, they can feel positive emotions and excitement, and it can encourage them to post more frequently (Stsiampkouskaya et al., 2021). Conversely, if they do not get the reaction as expected, they feel disappointment. This is closely connected to people’s desire for social approval.It particularly impacts youngsters, who are not yet mature enough to realise that social media is different from reality. <br>

Much of the content on social media highlights people’s lifestyles through their own selected photos and videos. Many users choose to show only the best aspects of their moments, with attractive photos, branded products, and personal achievements (Blease, 2015). When people see those contents repeatedly, they may become dissatisfied with their own lives because they unconsciously compare themselves to the poses they have seen. This comparison affects dissatisfaction, self-esteem, and overall psychological well-being. <br>

In some instances, overusing social media causes addiction and depression. The more use of social media platforms leads to greater exposure to symptoms of depression and anxiety. (Lin et al., 2016; Vannucci et al., 2017). Research shows that individuals who use more than 10 different social media platforms are more likely to have anxiety and depression symptoms than those who use two or fewer(Primack et al., 2017). An individual who spent an excessive amount of time on social media reported higher levels of loneliness and stress(Moreno et al., 2012).
As social media continues to evolve, algorithms are designed to capture users’ attention to stay online and encourage endless scrolling through their feeds without realising how much time has passed. Excessive social media use can significantly affect time management and daily performance. The increasing amount of time people spend on social media may reduced their valuable time for study or leisure activities. Spending more than 4 hours on social media leads to deviating from daily plans and other important responsibilities.<br>

There are several negative aspects of using social media, such as time consumption and seeking validing from others through media are negative affect people’s mental health. However, there are many benefits of social media, such as instant news being available. And connect with people easily, regardless of the distance between them. One study shows that Facebook use is associated with increased connectedness and decreases in depression. Overall, there are some negative points of social media use; however, when monitored and used appropriately, it may help reduce depression and improve other mood disorders.<br>


## Ethical Reflection & Improvements
Social media has developed significantly from MySpace to Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok. These platfroms have made communication easier and allow people to share information, photos and videos worldwide. However their development has also created ethical issues including privacy concerns, cyberbullying, and negative effects on mental health. Critically evaluating social media shows that although it has many benefits improvements are needed to protect users.

Benefits of Social Media
Social media has clear advantages. It helps people maintain relationships across distance, gives small businesses and creators a global audience at low cost, and lets people find communities they could not reach offline. Research on young people also shows that online connection can protect well-being in some contexts. (Agyapong-Opoku et al). (2025) found mixed results during the COVID-19 pandemic, with neutral, negative, and positive effects all reported. A fair evaluation must acknowledge these benefits before examining the harms.

Ethical Concerns
Mental health.
(Agyapong-Opoku et al). (2025) concluded that the impact of social media on adolescent mental health is complex and shaped by moderating factors. Their review also associated problematic patterns of use, rather than use itself, with harm. (Cabezas-Klinger et al). (2025) likewise synthesised recent evidence linking social media use to mental disorders and suicidal ideation risk in adolescents and young adults. Together, these studies suggest that harm is real but uneven, depending on how, how much, and by whom platforms are used. This supports targeted protections, especially for children, rather than blanket claims that social media is harmful

Cyberbullying and online safety.
Constant connectivity and anonymity make harassment easier to carry out and harder to escape. Because abuse is often reported to platforms rather than to a person with authority, slow removal of harmful content raises questions about corporate responsibility.

Privacy and manipulative design.
Platforms profit from collecting behavioural data to target advertising, and users rarely understand how much is gathered. European regulators have highlighted deceptive design practices and shortcomings in advertising transparency on major platforms. These practices show that ethical problems come from business models and design choices, not only from individual users' behaviour.

Improvements Needed
Protecting users requires action from governments, companies, and users themselves. Stronger and enforced regulation. Laws are beginning to move from principle to enforcement. Australia's Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act requires platforms to take reasonable steps to prevent under-16s from holding accounts, with penalties of up to AUD 109.2 million. The obligation took effect in December 2025 (eSafety Commissioner, 2026), and eSafety has begun a formal evaluation of its real-world impact. Other countries can learn from the results, including concerns about privacy and age-verification accuracy.

Transparency and oversight.
Platforms should explain how recommendation systems work and give vetted researchers access to data. Faster responses to harassment and clear appeals processes would also build trust.
