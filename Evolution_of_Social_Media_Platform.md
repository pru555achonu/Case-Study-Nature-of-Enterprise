## Introduction

*First view point analysis:*

*Societal view point analysis:*

Social media has evolved from personal communication into an essential aspect of people’s lives. Research shows that 4.88 billion people use social media worldwide, representing 60.6% of the global population. The most popular social media platforms include Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok, which have at least one billion active users monthly(Kemp, 2023).<br> 

Social media platforms facilitate users many opportunities such as expressing their identity, connecting with people, interacting with others, validating by blue marks or likes from followers. In addition, people can connect regardless of geographical distance or different time zones, which is crucial for social interaction (Kietzmann et al., 2011). These benefits make users enjoy using social media. Many people have become social influencers and entrepreneurs by taking advantage of these opportunities, earning income through social media platforms in ways that were not possible in the past.<br>

Although social media offers many benefits, it can also have a negative impact on society if the user is not using it wisely. It can psychologically influence people's sense of belonging and validation(Nadkarni & Hofmann, 2012). People often seek validation in the form of blue marks or likes from followers. Many studies show that the number of likes and comments can influence people in various ways, such as emotions, self-esteem, and social behaviour. For instance, when the poster receives high numbers of likes and comments, they can feel positive emotions and excitement, and it can encourage them to post more frequently (Stsiampkouskaya et al., 2021). Conversely, if they do not get the reaction as expected, they feel disappointment. This is closely connected to people’s desire for social approval.<br>

Much of the content on social media highlights people’s lifestyles through their own selected photos and videos. Many users choose to show only the best aspects of their moments, with attractive photos, branded products, and personal achievements (Blease, 2015). When people see those contents repeatedly, they may become dissatisfied with their own lives because they unconsciously compare themselves to the poses they have seen. This comparison affects dissatisfaction, self-esteem, and overall psychological well-being. <br>

In some instances, overusing social media causes addiction and depression. The more use of social media platforms leads to greater exposure to symptoms of depression and anxiety. (Lin et al., 2016; Vannucci et al., 2017). Research shows that individuals who use more than 10 different social media platforms are more likely to have anxiety and depression symptoms than those who use two or fewer(Primack et al., 2017). An individual who spent an excessive amount of time on social media reported higher levels of loneliness and stress(Moreno et al., 2012).
As social media continues to evolve, algorithms are designed to capture users’ attention to stay online and encourage endless scrolling through their feeds without realising how much time has passed. Excessive social media use can significantly affect individuals’  time management and daily performance. It can consume users’ valuable time, leaving them missing their study time or leisure activities. Spending more than 4 hours on social media leads to deviating from daily plans and other important responsibilities.<br>

There are several negative aspects of using social media, such as time consumption and seeking validing from others through media are negative affect people’s mental health. However, there are many benefits of social media, such as instant news being available. And connect with people easily, regardless of the distance between them. One study shows that Facebook use is associated with increased connectedness and decreases in depression. Overall, there are some negative points of social media use; however, when monitored and used appropriately, it may help reduce depression and improve other mood disorders.<br>


## Ethical Reflection & Improvements
Social media has developed significantly from MySpace to Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok. These platfroms have made communication easier and allow people to share information, photos and videos worldwide. However their development has also created ethical issues including privacy concerns, cyberbullying, and negative effects on mental health. Critically evaluating social media shows that although it has many benefits improvements are needed to protect users.

### Benefits of Social Media
Social media has clear advantages. It helps people maintain relationships across distance, gives small businesses and creators a global audience at low cost, and lets people find communities they could not reach offline. Research on young people also shows that online connection can protect well-being in some contexts. (Agyapong-Opoku et al). (2025) found mixed results during the COVID-19 pandemic, with neutral, negative, and positive effects all reported. A fair evaluation must acknowledge these benefits before examining the harms.

### Ethical Concerns
### Mental health.
(Agyapong-Opoku et al). (2025) concluded that the impact of social media on adolescent mental health is complex and shaped by moderating factors. Their review also associated problematic patterns of use, rather than use itself, with harm. (Cabezas-Klinger et al). (2025) likewise synthesised recent evidence linking social media use to mental disorders and suicidal ideation risk in adolescents and young adults. Together, these studies suggest that harm is real but uneven, depending on how, how much, and by whom platforms are used. This supports targeted protections, especially for children, rather than blanket claims that social media is harmful

### Cyberbullying and online safety.
Constant connectivity and anonymity make harassment easier to carry out and harder to escape. Because abuse is often reported to platforms rather than to a person with authority, slow removal of harmful content raises questions about corporate responsibility.

### Privacy and manipulative design.
Platforms profit from collecting behavioural data to target advertising, and users rarely understand how much is gathered. European regulators have highlighted deceptive design practices and shortcomings in advertising transparency on major platforms. These practices show that ethical problems come from business models and design choices, not only from individual users' behaviour.

### Improvements Needed
Protecting users requires action from governments, companies, and users themselves. Stronger and enforced regulation. Laws are beginning to move from principle to enforcement. Australia's Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act requires platforms to take reasonable steps to prevent under-16s from holding accounts, with penalties of up to AUD 109.2 million. The obligation took effect in December 2025 (eSafety Commissioner, 2026), and eSafety has begun a formal evaluation of its real-world impact. Other countries can learn from the results, including concerns about privacy and age-verification accuracy.

### Transparency and oversight.
Platforms should explain how recommendation systems work and give vetted researchers access to data. Faster responses to harassment and clear appeals processes would also build trust.

### Conclusion
Social media has transformed communication, but its development has been shaped by commercial incentives that often conflict with user well-being. Recent evidence suggests platforms are neither simply good nor bad. Their impact depends on design, regulation, and patterns of use. Enforceable laws, ethically designed products, independent oversight, and education can preserve the benefits of social media while reducing risks to privacy, safety, and mental health.
