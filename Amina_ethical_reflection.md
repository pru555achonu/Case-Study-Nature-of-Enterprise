# Ethical Reflection & Improvements

Social media has developed significantly from MySpace to Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok. These platfroms have made communication easier and
allow people to share information, photos and videos worldwide.
However their development has also created ethical issues including privacy concerns, cyberbullying, and negative effects on mental health. 
Critically evaluating social media shows that although it has many benefits improvements are needed to protect users.

## Benefits of Social Media

Social media has clear advantages. It helps people maintain relationships across distance, gives small businesses and creators a global audience at low cost, and lets people find communities they could not reach offline. 
Research on young people also shows that online connection can protect well-being in some contexts. 
(Agyapong-Opoku et al). (2025) found mixed results during the COVID-19 pandemic, with neutral, negative, and positive effects all reported. A fair evaluation must acknowledge these benefits before examining the harms.

## Ethical Concerns
Mental health.

(Agyapong-Opoku et al). (2025) concluded that the impact of social media on adolescent mental health is complex and shaped by moderating factors.
Their review also associated problematic patterns of use, rather than use itself, with harm.
(Cabezas-Klinger et al). (2025) likewise synthesised recent evidence linking social media use to mental disorders and suicidal ideation risk in adolescents and young adults. 
Together, these studies suggest that harm is real but uneven, depending on how, how much, and by whom platforms are used. This supports targeted protections, especially for children, rather than blanket claims that social media is harmful

## Cyberbullying and online safety.

Constant connectivity and anonymity make harassment easier to carry out and harder to escape.
Because abuse is often reported to platforms rather than to a person with authority, slow removal of harmful content raises questions about corporate responsibility.

## Privacy and manipulative design.

Platforms profit from collecting behavioural data to target advertising, and users rarely understand how much is gathered. 
European regulators have highlighted deceptive design practices and shortcomings in advertising transparency on major platforms.
These practices show that ethical problems come from business models and design choices, not only from individual users' behaviour. 

## Accountability and transparency.

In December 2025 the European Commission fined X €120 million under the Digital Services Act, partly because it created unnecessary barriers to researchers accessing public data.
This was the first non-compliance decision under the Act. 
Without independent access to data, society cannot test what platforms claim about their own safety.

## Improvements Needed

Protecting users requires action from governments, companies, and users themselves.
Stronger and enforced regulation. Laws are beginning to move from principle to enforcement.
Australia's Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act requires platforms to take reasonable steps to prevent under-16s from holding accounts, with penalties of up to AUD 109.2 million.
The obligation took effect in December 2025 (eSafety Commissioner, 2026), and eSafety has begun a formal evaluation of its real-world impact.
Other countries can learn from the results, including concerns about privacy and age-verification accuracy. 

## Transparency and oversight.
Platforms should explain how recommendation systems work and give vetted researchers access to data. 
Faster responses to harassment and clear appeals processes would also build trust.

## Conclusion
Social media has transformed communication, but its development has been shaped by commercial incentives that often conflict with user well-being.
Recent evidence suggests platforms are neither simply good nor bad. Their impact depends on design, regulation, and patterns of use. Enforceable laws, ethically designed products, 
independent oversight, and education can preserve the benefits of social media while reducing risks to privacy, safety, and mental health.
